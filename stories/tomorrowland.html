Tomorrowland: Tickets, Logistics, My Experience

How to actually get tickets, what it costs, and why I ended up moshing in Tevas.
Avanti·July 2024·16 min read

Tomorrowland is one of the biggest music festivals in the world — a bucket list event for a lot of people. I'd heard of it and knew it would be special, but what I didn't realize was just how much fun I was going to have.

This was my first major music festival — and my first EDM festival — and let me just say: it did not disappoint. I'm already plotting my return. (Tomorrowland 2026, who's coming with me??)

✦ ✦ ✦

Tickets & Logistics

Here's the first thing you need to know: tickets sell out instantly. You have to pre-register, and even then, the odds can feel slim.

How it works: If you've been before, you get earlier access, which makes it easier to keep booking year after year. But since my friends and I were newbies, we were raw-dogging it. We signed up for presale, woke up early, and crossed our fingers.

Pro Tip:
  • If you're going with a group, make sure everyone tries. I personally never made it into the ticket portal, but thankfully, a couple of my friends did, and they secured tickets for all of us. We decided to book the Global Journey Pass, which included transportation into Belgium, accommodations, entry to the festival, and shuttle service to and from our hotel.

My thoughts: For first-timers, Global Journey was a great choice. It took a lot of stress out of the process. But next time? I'm camping.

Here's why: Tomorrowland takes place in a small town called Boom, about an hour from Brussels. With Global Journey, we stayed in a hostel-style hotel in the city, which meant it took forever to get to and from the festival. Each night, it was a 40-minute walk from the festival grounds to the shuttles, then another hour-long ride back. By the time we got home, any energy we had left for an after-party was long gone. And as someone with a tiny bladder? Not my favorite setup.

Camping puts you right on-site and fully immersed in the experience. No death marches to shuttles, no two-hour commute — just vibes.

That said, Global Journey was still well-rounded. You can upgrade to higher-tier hotels if you want something fancier, and for about $1,200 total, it covered almost everything: festival entry, accommodations, shuttles, and even trains from bigger cities where flights land. For first-timers, it's a safe bet.

My Experience at Tomorrowland 2024

OMG. Where do I even start?

The production value is insane. The amount of time and effort put into the stages is unreal. There were over 12 unique stages, each with its own theme — some felt like underground dungeons, others gave Tulum beach club vibes. One was designed like a library (which was one of my faves).

The main stage changes every year, and in 2023, the theme was "Life". It was a massive, intricate Garden of Eden setup with vines and flowers. It was breathtaking.

Here's the thing: before Tomorrowland, I wasn't a huge EDM or house listener. But it didn't matter. Between the big-name artists and the sheer energy of the place, I was having the time of my life. Every single day somehow topped the one before it.

And the crowd? Honestly one of the best parts. Tomorrowland draws people from all over the world, across all ages, and everyone was so kind. I even moshed (in Tevas, I was wilding) during DJ Diesel (aka Shaq). And unlike what I knew about mosh pits, this one was civil: if someone fell, people stopped to pick them up. If something got lost, people made sure it was returned. My friend lost her phone in that pit and got it back. Unreal.

Another thing that made the whole experience stress-free: finding our crew was never an issue. I have T-Mobile, so I had international data the whole time, and it worked perfectly across the festival grounds. Being able to text and share locations made it super easy to regroup, even with the massive crowds.

Oh — and the food. You wouldn't expect festival food to be gourmet, but Tomorrowland delivers. I had meals there better than some restaurants I've ever eaten at.

Overall, the festival was so well-organized. From the flow of the grounds to the diversity of the stages to the logistics of moving massive crowds — it just worked.

✦ ✦ ✦

Tomorrowland's motto is Live Today, Love Tomorrow, Unite Forever. And you don't just see that plastered on banners, you actually feel it. It's in the music, the energy of the stages, the way strangers take care of each other in the crowd.

I went into this trip expecting a cool weekend with friends. What I got was something bigger: a reminder of how powerful it feels to be surrounded by people from every corner of the world, all just there to celebrate and connect.

Even if you don't know the music well (like me going in), you'll still find your space. With 12+ stages and so many artists, there's always a pocket of energy that fits your vibe, but the real magic isn't just the sets or the stages — it's the community.

Tomorrowland showed me how music can bring people together in a way that feels universal. You're not just at a festival: you're part of something that transcends borders, languages, and backgrounds.

The feeling really does go on and on and on. Fittingly, that was my song of the festival. (If you haven't heard it, check out Lost Frequencies.)

Would I go back? Absolutely! I can't wait to bring my sister so she can feel that same magic too. (AND I AM!)

Tomorrowland is DEF worth the hype.
