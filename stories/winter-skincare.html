Winter Skincare Guide

Winter, But for Your Face

The winter travel skincare I trust after Vienna, Copenhagen, eight NYC winters and the mountains.
Avanti·Skincare guide·10 min read

Two weeks into studying abroad in Vienna, my face staged a full-blown rebellion.

Between the wind, the indoor heating that never seemed to turn off and air that felt like it was pulling moisture out of everything I owned—including me—my skin was suddenly tight, flaky and doing things I hadn’t seen it do since middle school.

That trip taught me that winter travel isn’t just cold. It’s skin-cold. And the routine that gets you through summer in Austin will not necessarily get you through December in Vienna.

Since then, I’ve spent a winter working in Copenhagen, lived through eight NYC winters and picked up snowboarding—which introduced my face to an entirely new combination of cold, wind, altitude and sunlight reflecting off snow. Through a lot of trial and error, I’ve learned what my skin actually needs, what earns space in my toiletry bag, and what I can leave at home.

For context, I have normal-to-combination skin. I’m also not a dermatologist, and this is not a universal skincare routine. It’s what has worked for me after years of traveling and living in cold places. I’ll explain why I reach for each product, but your skin may need something completely different.

I’m also not bringing a twelve-step routine on every winter trip. These are simply the products I trust to keep my face, lips, hands and body from feeling completely dried out by day two.

What I Actually Change in Winter

Cold weather does not automatically mean you need more products. For me, it means I need to be more intentional about the products I’m already using.

Cold air, wind and low indoor humidity can make it harder for skin to hold onto moisture. That is when mine starts feeling tight, flaky or suddenly irritated by products it normally tolerates. Instead of trying to fix that by piling on ten new steps, I make my routine gentler and more protective.

When I’m actually being disciplined, this is the order I follow:

  • 0. Oil cleanser: In a perfect world, I would do this every day. In reality, I mainly use an oil cleanser to remove my makeup before following it with my regular cleanser.
  • 1. Gentle cleanser: Nothing overly stripping or aggressive. If my face feels tight immediately after washing it, the cleanser is not helping.
  • 2. Hydrating toner, essence or serum: I don’t necessarily use all three. I choose one or two hydration-focused layers and pull back on stronger acids when my skin already feels irritated.
  • 3. A richer moisturizer: This is the first product I upgrade for winter because it helps keep the hydration underneath from disappearing.
  • 4. A sleeping mask when needed: Not every night—just when my skin feels like it needs extra help.
  • 5. Sunscreen in the morning: Yes, even in the winter. Yes, every day! It becomes especially important when I’ll be outside for hours or around reflective snow.

This may be overkill for a normal day, but winter travel is not always a normal day. You’re moving between cold streets, overheated buildings, airplanes and hotel rooms where you have no control over the thermostat. My goal is not to build the longest possible routine. It is to keep my skin comfortable enough that I don’t have to think about it for the rest of the trip.

The ILLIYOON Ceramide Ato Concentrate Cream is my go-to winter moisturizer. It gives me the hydration I need during colder months without feeling overly thick or heavy on my skin, which is surprisingly difficult to find in a richer moisturizer.

The UV Rays Are Still Raying

Sunscreen is a must even when you cannot see the sun. The UV rays are, unfortunately, still raying—and snow reflects sunlight, making sun protection especially important during long days on the mountain.

A quick explanation of the labels: SPF primarily tells you about protection from the UV rays associated with sunburn, while the PA rating used on many Asian sunscreens indicates the level of UVA protection. You don’t need to become a sunscreen scientist, but I look for strong UVA and UVB protection in something I will realistically wear every day.

My Everyday Favorite: THANK YOU FARMER Sun Project Water Sun Cream

My number-one everyday sunscreen is the THANK YOU FARMER Sun Project Water Sun Cream SPF 50+ PA+++. I’ve been using it for a couple of years now and still love it.

It feels more like part of my skincare routine than a heavy sunscreen I have to convince myself to wear. That matters because the sunscreen you enjoy applying consistently is more useful than the technically perfect one you keep avoiding.

My Second Choice: Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun

The Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun: Rice + Probiotics SPF 50+ PA++++ is a close second for me. It is another option I trust for everyday wear and regularly keep in my sunscreen rotation.

I also like Supergoop! Unseen Sunscreen, particularly when I want something that works well with makeup or is easy to find before a trip.

Everyday Sunscreen Is Not Automatically Mountain Sunscreen

For snowboarding or another full day outdoors, I look for a water-resistant formula and try to follow the reapplication instructions on the product. Sweat, snow and wiping your face can all interfere with the layer you applied that morning.

I also make sure to cover the easy-to-forget areas: ears, neck, hairline and any exposed skin around my goggles. Your skincare cannot protect you if it is sitting in the hotel room.

When I Bring a Face Mask

If my skin is really dry and I have enough space in my row—or I’m sitting in the aisle and can easily get up—I may do a sheet mask during the flight.

Most of the time, though, I save it for an airport lounge during a longer layover. If I have time, I’ll brush my teeth, wash my face and do a little skincare reset there. It is much easier than trying to manage an entire routine from an airplane seat while the person beside me is eating pretzels.

What I Leave at Home

Most skincare lists only tell you what to add. Mine also contains a removal section because I am not packing my entire bathroom for a five-day trip.

I usually leave behind:

  • Anything I have never tried before. A winter trip is not the time to discover that your skin hates a new active—especially when you are on the wrong continent.
  • Strong exfoliants when my skin is already dry or irritated. If the cold and wind are doing enough, I do not need to contribute to the problem.
  • Retinol when my skin needs a break. I do not automatically stop using it for the entire winter, but I may skip it around a long flight or when my skin is already feeling sensitive.
  • Every nonessential step in my at-home routine. I am not bringing multiple serums and a full ten-step routine to perform at 6 a.m. in a Copenhagen hostel bathroom.
  • Products that are annoying to pack. If something is bulky, likely to leak or requires an entire decanting project, I either find a smaller alternative or leave it at home.

My winter travel routine is less about doing everything perfectly and more about keeping my skin comfortable. A few reliable products I will actually use are more helpful than a toiletry bag full of good intentions.

Two weeks into Vienna, I thought my skin had simply decided it hated Europe. It turns out it was reacting to the same things I was: the wind, the cold, the indoor heating, and the general shock of leaving a Texas winter for an actual one.

After Vienna wind, Copenhagen darkness, eight NYC winters and several days on the mountain, my routine is still not elaborate. It is just better prepared.

You do not need every product on this list, and your skin may need something completely different from mine. Start with what you already know works, add hydration where you need it and protect your skin before it reaches the full-blown-rebellion stage.

Apparently, I did not hate winter—and neither did my face. We were both just wildly unprepared for it.
